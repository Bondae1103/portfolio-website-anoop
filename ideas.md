## Chosen Approach: Fossil Signal

### Design Movement
Retrofuturism with industrial wayfinding: a hybrid of mid-century technical illustration, analog computer terminals, and cinematic science-fiction production design.

### Core Principles
1. **Field instrument, not brochure.** Every section should feel like a readable research console or specimen record.
2. **Warm signal against engineered darkness.** Amber, bone, and oxidized green create a human, biological counterpoint to graphite and near-black surfaces.
3. **Evidence-led storytelling.** Metrics, dates, tools, and outcomes appear as data labels and annotations rather than vague claims.
4. **Tension through restraint.** Use hazard markers, scanlines, and subtle motion sparingly so the interface feels intentional rather than noisy.

### Color Philosophy
The background is deep graphite with a faint green cast, like a dim lab or spacecraft interior. The signature color is **amber signal #F2B84B**, chosen to evoke CRT phosphor, warning tape, and the glow of an instrument still powered on. Bone-white text keeps the page legible and archival, while oxidized fern green and rust red separate biological content from system warnings.

### Layout Paradigm
A cinematic single-page dossier with an offset content rail: a persistent left-side mission index on desktop, a wide evidence column, and occasional full-bleed specimen bands. Sections do not sit in identical centered cards; they alternate between annotated panels, timeline strips, and split compositions.

### Signature Elements
- A vertical **MISSION INDEX** rail with numbered anchors and a blinking status marker.
- **Specimen label** chips, coordinates, timestamps, and data-quality tags attached to content blocks.
- Fine scanline texture, clipped corner brackets, and thin amber rules that suggest technical hardware without overwhelming the copy.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like operating a real console: links jump to a labeled section, project cards reveal their evidence, and buttons use clear action language. Hover states brighten the amber signal and expose metadata; placeholder project links explicitly identify themselves as awaiting deployment URLs.

### Animation
Use short, snappy opacity and transform transitions under 280ms. The hero signal marker gets a slow, low-contrast pulse; project cards lift by a few pixels on hover; section labels reveal with a subtle 30–60ms stagger. No large parallax or constant motion. Respect reduced-motion preferences by disabling the scan pulse and entrance transforms.

### Typography System
Use **Space Mono** for labels, metadata, navigation, and data readouts. Use **Barlow Condensed** for display headlines and **IBM Plex Sans** for body copy. Display headings use tight tracking, uppercase fragments, and occasional italic emphasis; body text stays at a comfortable 1.65 line height.

### Brand Essence
A computational biology portfolio for teams building the next generation of data-driven life science tools—different because it treats biology, code, and communication as one integrated system.

**Personality:** Investigative, precise, quietly bold.

### Brand Voice
Headlines sound like field notes with a point of view. CTAs are direct and specific; microcopy explains what the visitor is looking at instead of using generic filler.

Example lines:
- “Turning noisy biological systems into signals worth following.”
- “Open the case file — project links are staged for their next deployment.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark is a custom stacked lockup reading **ANOOP / NAIR**, set in condensed uppercase with a broken amber scan line passing through the slash. The mark is a simple **double-helix / orbital-bracket symbol**: two offset arcs contained in a square specimen frame, with no text, designed to work as a favicon and section stamp.

### Signature Brand Color
**Amber Signal — #F2B84B**

This color should appear in the logo, active navigation, key metrics, call-to-action borders, and the primary scanline motif.
